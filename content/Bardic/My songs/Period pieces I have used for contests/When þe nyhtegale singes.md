# Lyrics
The lyrics as Danperia gave them to me:

*When the nyhtegale singes,*
*The wodes waxen grene,*
*Lef ant gras ant blosme springes*
*In Averyl, Y wene;*
*Ant love is to myn herte gon*
*With one spere so kene,*
*Nyht ant day my blod hit drynkes*
*Myn herte deth me tene.*

*Ich have loved al this yer*
*That Y may love na more;*
*Ich have siked moni syk,*
*Lemmon, for thin ore,*
*Me nis love neuer the ner,*
*Ant that me reweth sore;*
*Suete lemmon, thench on me,*
*Ich have loved the yore.*

*Suete lemmon, Y preye thee,*
*Of love one speche;*
*Whil Y lyve in world so wyde*
*Other nulle Y seche.*
*With thy love, my suete leof,*
*My blis thou mihtes eche;*
*A suete cos of thy mouth*
*Mihte be my leche.*

*Suete lemmon, Y preye thee*
*Of a love-bene:*
*Yef thou me lovest, ase men says,*
*Lemmon, as I wene,*
*Ant yef hit thi wille be,*
*Thou loke that hit be sene;*
*So muchel Y thenke vpon the*
*That al y waxe grene.*

*Bituene Lyncolne ant Lyndeseye,*
*Northamptoun ant Lounde,*
*Ne wot I non so fayr a may,*
*As y go fore ybounde.*
*Suete lemmon, Y preye the*
*Thou lovie me a stounde;*
*Y wole mone my song*
*On wham that hit ys on ylong.*
# phonetic lyrics
To make it easier for me to learn, I edited the lyrics she gave me to better match the sounds of the recording she sent me of herself singing them

*When the neetengalyh sings*
*The wodes waxen greene,*
*Leaf and grass and blosum spring*
*In Averyl, I ween;*
*And love is to mine heart-e gone*
*With one-e spear so keene,*
*Night and day my blood it drynkes*
*Mine hearte doth me tene.*

*I have lov-ed all this year*
*That I may love na more;*
*I have sighed a moni sigh,*
*Le-mon, for thine ore,*
*Yet your love seemes neuer ner,*
*And that me reweth sore;*
*Su-ete le-mon, think on me,*
*I have-e loved the yore.*

*Su-ete le-mon, I preye thee,*
*Of lov-ed one speche;*
*While I live in world so wide*
*Other none I see.*
*With thy love, my su-ete be-lon,*
*My blis thou ,mightes eche;*
*A su-ete cays of thy mouth*
*Mihte be my lech.*

*Su-ete le-mon, I pray-e thee*
*Of a love-token:*
*Yef thou me lovest, as men says,*
*Le-mon, as I ween,*
*And if hit thy wille be,*
*Thou look that hit be seene;*
*So much do I thinke upon the*
*That all I wax-e grene.*

*Bituene Lyncolne ant Lyndeseye,*
*Norhamptoun and Lounde,*
*Ne wot I non so fayr a maaid*
*As she to whom I'm bound.*
*Suete lemmon, Y preye the*
*Thy lovie must me astounde;*
*lest I forever morne my song*
*On whom it doth beylong.*
# Documentation
The manuscript with the original tex is available at: http://www.bl.uk/catalogues/illuminatedmanuscripts/record.asp?MSID=7704&CollID=8&NStart=2253 https://catalogue.bl.uk/permalink/44BL_MAIN/19agrh4/alma990141612210109251

The manuscript is called **Harley 2253**
**Author** Henri d'Acre
**Title**	Various texts including secular and religious lyrics (also known as the 'Harley Lyrics'), including King Horn and the Follies of Fashion, and Psalms listed for recitation at certain times (imperfect)
**Origin**	England, Central (Hereford, Ludlow)
**Date**	1st half of the 14th century
**Language**	French, Latin, and English (for the whole manuscript, but this song is Middle English)

Here are two published posts with lyrics and translation: 
- https://www.luminarium.org/medlit/medlyric/nyhtegale.php
- https://aclerkofoxford.blogspot.com/2011/04/medieval-love-poem.html
