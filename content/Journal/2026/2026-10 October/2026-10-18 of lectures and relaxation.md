# into town for a lecture
The Skellefteå Museum had a lecture on the big 1932 strike and ensuing conflict at Klemensnäs, a woodpulp/paper mill just outside of Skellefteå. The speaker, Maja Östlund, has [recently completed a master's thesis on the subject](https://urn.kb.se/resolve?urn=urn%3Anbn%3Ase%3Aumu%3Adiva-254785). We went because Keldor is especially interested in the "*arbetarrörelsen*" (Labour movement) that led to the 1938 *Saltsjöbadsavtalet*, which created the "Swedish Model" of regular agreements between unions and employers that pretty much ended the need for workers in Sweden to strike. The 1932 incident that the lecture focused on was one of several in that period in which either police (in this case), or military opened fire on the demonstrators. There were more than 1000 people demonstrating here that day, but, luckily, while a few people where hit (including a glancing blow to one guy's temple), none died. Keldor says that his grandfather and granddad's brother were both in the crowd, though they were too young to have been among the workers who were striking due to huge cuts in pay. (given that Keldor's dad wasn't born till 1944, the fact that Keldor knows his dad's dad was there that day says rather a lot about the local significance of the conflict). One of the noteworthy parts of the conflict was the *"vattenspruteindicenten"*, where the reporter who was trying to get into the factory to take photos of the strike breakers was washed away by the high pressure water hose, which triggered the escalation of violence that transformed the demonstration into a riot, and, in the long run, helped lead to the *Saltsjöbadsavtalet*.
