# my non-genderness
A friend asked this morning if my pronouns were correct in a paragraph about me, which had  "she" in the text, and I replied

> "I prefer they to she".

To which I got the  reply:

>"Apologies, will do better at remembering that!"

To which I replied:

> "No worries, I didn't change pronouns till sometime in the middle of the last gap in hanging out with you"

(There having elapsed som years since last we had been in contact). This morning I woke up to a new question:

> "So at one point you were non binary but went with she/her?"

And typed this reply:

No, I have bern nonbinairy all my life, I just didn't know it was a thing, and only used she because I was told when I was little that I was a girl, and that she was the word used for girls.

In the 80's, when Men are from Mars and Women are from Venus came out (which I never read), people started quoting it, ir talking about gender differences,  and, often, I would say "that isn't true, if it were, I would be a man". But, as I hadn't heard of non binary as a concept applied to Human gender, I thought no mare about it.

But from the first day a hint of breast started growing, I hated them with a passion, and wished them gone. From the first time I started menstruating, I hated it with a passion, and wanted it gone.

Part of the reason I entered my second childhood was never wanting to be a woman. Everthing I had ever heard about being a woman sounded terrible (they have kids, they are the one who does the nurturing, they are supposed to be timid and no sense of adventure, their rights have  been traditionally limited by gender , etc, etc, etc.)

But I deeply and truly didn't want to be a man and was so grateful I had no penius or testicles, as everthing I had heard about that sounded horrid (ok, other than easier to pee in the mountains, when wearing a climbing harness).

But I still hadn't heard of nin binary,  nor of trans men. I had tead World According to Garp in high school,, so I knew it was possible for a man to remove his penis and become a woman, but I had no understanding of why anyone would wver want to be a woman, if they could be anything else, and was decades before I ever heard of a trans woman in another context, and far longer before I heard of the concept of trans men, and, again, I had zero understanding of why one would choose to be a man, of one could  be anything else.

But I thought for years that the only other option was child, so that is, and always has been, how I identify. 

It was well into the 2000's that I first heard non-binary, and then the context was more on the "both" end of the spectrum than the "neither", and I didn't pay much attention,  as I didn't want to be either a man or a woman.

When I first heard "they/them" used as a preferred pronoun, my thought was "I always wanted to be twins (of the sort in fiction woth a telepathic connection, so it is more one person with two bodies), and using "they" would rub my nose into the part where there i only one of me", so I didn't go there.

So I didn't choose any pronouns in the early days of adding pronouns to one's name, and let folk go with the default. 

Then, the more I thought about it, the more I wanted to disassociate myself from the concept of woman, but I still didn't want they because I still was sad that I have only one body, I should have another. So I started saying that all pronouns are ok, even it (which I secretly preferred, but enough people find offensive when used with people, I didn't suggest it).

It was only when politics started getting weird in the states and other places that there was much anti trans stuff in the news tgat I switched from any/all to they/them, and now that is what shows on all socisal media i have remembered to update, and it is also on my work profile. (though getting a PhD was partially motivated by rhe fact that I could use Dr. Instead of Ms, Mrs, or Miss)
