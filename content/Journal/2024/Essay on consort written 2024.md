
# question from Agnés
ETA: Please read the post. I'm asking for contributions from female and NB fighters. If you're neither, this question is not for you at this point.

Question for my female and NB fighter friends: how do you approach the SCA consort institution, which is fundamentally founded upon the binary, heteronormative concept of the active male advancing the station of the passive female, whose admiration is required for the male fighter to do their best?
# my reply
I was a consort long before I ever tried putting on armour, and inspired my then-boyfriend to victory in the 2nd Oerthan Principality tournament when I was 18 years old. He had been knighted only two weeks before, and then sat me down to explain that his goal for the summer had been to either be knighted or win Coronet, and as he had achieved the goal, he wasn’t going to push for the tournament, too, and I assured him that was ok.

Even well before the day we won I had heard many songs and stories about how fighters need a consort to inspire them, and that having that consort really and truly made a difference to how they fought, and their odds of winning. 

I have a short attention span, and when I don’t have a champion fighting for me, I wind up talking to people, or doing something, and never remember to watch the fighting. When I have a champion on the field, however, my place is by their side, and I give them full focus. I help them into armour (and help with last minute repairs and adjustments), I see to it that they have shade, water, food, and anything else they need, and I walk with them to the field before every fight. Then the best part happens: the salutes.  That moment when our eyes meet across the field, and we both draw in that sudden sharp intake of breath from which I believe the word “inspiration” comes, as we truly inspire, or take in air at that moment. That connection is one where most of my champions have used to set themselves and pull in the focus that lets them do their best in the fight, so, in a very real sense, it really is “us” on the field, and I watch every blow. 

I haven’t ever needed to do it, but I would be prepared to call a hold if I saw my champion shrugging off a blow that should have been good, and ask them directly how it felt, and why they didn’t call it, because it isn’t only their honour on the line, and I am too deeply intrenched in the honour system of our game to be willing to have someone play “rhino” when they fight for me.

In my 42 years in the SCA I have heard many, many dukes, counts, viscounts, knights, and even unbelted fighters speaking of the difference it makes to have the right consort—that they truly fight better on the day if they are carrying the favour of someone they care about and are inspired by (note: this does not need to be romantic—I have also heard people say this about their friends, their parents, and their siblings). 

In all these years I have never, ever interpreted the fighter consort relationship in the active-passive terms that you mentioned here, though I understand where that would come from, as a fair few medieval philosophers do describe the male-female dichotomy as active and passive, especially as pertains to sexual intercourse. However, I have also heard enough people laughing off that particular philosophy and giving good examples as to why it just isn’t a good description of reality, that I am not surprised I never thought to extend it to our game.  

Perhaps because our fighter-consort relationships are entered into deliberately (even if one is “only” fighting for one’s current lover, both parties still have to decide if one will enter the tournament together or not), and it is not like the slimy knight in the Knights Tale movie who simply announces to a lady that he will defeat people for her, without so much as a “by your leave”.

After some years of being a consort I wound up with a champion who loves to encourage others to fight, and who had a workshop for helping people make armour. With his encouragement I finally did that which I had been secretly hoping to do for years, but never dared ask: put on armour and enter the tournament myself.  For me, entering the tournament as a pair is taking everything to the next level. We still get to share the salutes, but now I get to both stand on the sidelines, where I can see the blows as they come in, and on the field, where everything happens so fast, I can only try to throw blows and to block for as long as possible, and then (probably) die gloriously on the filed for my consort or (occasionally), land a blow for the honour of my consort. Either way, it is fun.

I have fought for and been fought for by variety of romantic partners over the years, and also for people who are “just friends”, but for whom I feel a love and whom inspire me.  I strongly recommend both. I am not certain I have any friends I don’t love, so I can’t speak to how it would feel to take the field for a friend I didn’t love…
