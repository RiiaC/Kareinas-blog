# My Food Log

When I enrolled in my PhD program in Tasmania, in 2005, i started keeping detailed logs of how I spent my time, so I would later be able to look it up if I ever wondered "*why isn't my thesis done yet?*"

Since I was tracking everything else, I eventually decided to also track my eating. But not the calorie counting approached favoured by people focused on weight loss. I am more interested in seeing how my diet is balanced in terms of types and amounts of food that I am eating.

Therefore I defined six catagories, and assigned everything I ate to one or more of these catagories, so I could watch if/how the percentages changed over time. 

Since I prefer not to eat factory food if I can avoid it, and strongly prefer my own cooking over restaurant food (being a fussy eater), it is pretty easy to keep track of what is in my food. On those occasions I eat something that someone else cooks I either ask for the recipe, or just guess the relative proportions of ingredients based on taste and appearance.

Here are the definitions I use for my catagories. Note that a food scientist or nutritionist would likely have done it very differently. 
## Protein
Around the same time I started my food log I also transitioned to mostly not eating meat, having figured out that the unpleasant after effects in my digestive tract didn't happen if I avoided eating store-bought meat. Therefore, this category mostly tracks the percentage of my food intake that is nuts, seeds, and beans, but on those rare occasions I eat something containing meat, it goes here, too. I also noticed the first year I started my food log journey that the cheese I normally bought stated on its ingredients list that it was 7% protein. Therefore, if I eat a solid cheese like cheddar, or the Swedish hushållsost, I record it as 93% dairy and 7% protein. But if I eat a softer cheese like ricotta or the Swedish färskost, I usually call it 100% dairy. 
## Dairy
Any real dairy product goes in here. I love cream, cheese, and butter, so I eat a fair bit of it. Note that as explained above, I typically track solid cheeses as 93% dairy and 7% protein  
## Starch
I love fresh baked bread, with plenty of butter. This category is for the bread part, or other grain based baked goods, and any other starchy things I eat, like rice, oatmeal, potatoes, pasta, etc. 
## Vegetable
In this catagory I record things that are commonly considered to be vegetables, so in addition to things like broccli and spinach, it also includes tomatos and avacodo. 
## Fruit
All of the fruits and berries I eat.
## Other
This is where I record anything that doesn't fall into the above categories, including: processed sugar, honey, non-dairy fat or oil used. (I normally only use oil if I am cooking for someone with a dairy allergy--I am one who loves butter, but dislikes the taste of vegetable oils. I don't even like the popular Swedish blend of butter and oil that is soft enough to spread straight from the fridge. I would rather eat my bread dry than use that). The category "other" also gets used on the rare occasion I eat factory food, especially "junk food"; I assign a percentage of that item to the other category to reflect the other sorts of additives that appear in such foods, sometimes as perseratives, or for other goals. Sometimes that percentage is based on a guess, sometimes I read the ingredients and adjust the other % up or down based on what I see there.
# Amounts
To keep the food log easy, I chose to measure by volume--how full was my bowl for that meal? My favourite bowl has rooms for around 2 measuring cups worth of food, or nearly 500 ml. Therefore, the log is recorded in terms of how many bowls (or fraction thereof) each item fills. (I have a strong preference to eat my food from a nice, deep, round bowl, that can comfortably be held in my hands, rather than a plate that is meant to sit on a table. This preference makes it easy to estimate how much I am eating at a time.)
To make converting a recipe easy, I set up a calculations sheet in my spreadsheet, in which I can simply add up ingredient amounts, and it converts it to percentages. For example, my [[Dandelion cookies#2023-03-19 Dandelion cookies, v3 large batch]] recipe converts to:

| ingredients                  | catagory | amount, converted to cups | percentage |
| ---------------------------- | -------- | ------------------------- | ---------- |
| 5 eggs, 1 1/3 c almond meal  | protein  | 1.96                      | 12%        |
| 620 g butter                 | dairy    | 2.73                      | 17%        |
| 2 c dandelion petals         | veg      | 2.00                      | 13%        |
|                              | fruit    |                           | 0%         |
| 8 c flour                    | starch   | 8.00                      | 51%        |
| 1 c sugar, 2 t baking powder | other    | 1.02                      | 6%         |
| **total**                        |          | **15.71**                     | **100%**       |
Note that when I bake cookies, I use a measuring tablespoon, to make all the cookies the same measured size, so one cookie translates to 0.03125 bowls of food, because there are 16 tablespoons in a cup, and 1/16 = 0.625, and my favourite bowl is big enough to hold 32 tablespoons of food.
